PERSONA TAXONOMY
A 66-Persona Framework for Industrial-Scale Notification Personalisation
Derived entirely from column-level schema (final_updated_main.csv) and the survey question / HPNS bucket structure — no individual user records were analysed to build this taxonomy.
1. Why 66 Personas, and How They're Built
This taxonomy is built by combining three independent axes, each grounded in a specific set of columns from the profile schema and the survey question structure, rather than in any observed user distribution. Multiplying the axes gives 60 combinatorial personas, plus 6 cross-cutting personas layered on top to handle edge cases the combinatorial grid doesn't cover cleanly (missing data, dormant signups, minority languages, and so on).
Axis 1 — Behavioural Segment (5 values): sourced from primary_category. This field is the same funnel classification used in the initial 5-persona framework.
Axis 2 — Life-Need Domain (3 values): sourced from the survey's HPNS structure (Health / Skills / Agriculture) — the three domains the questionnaire is organised into.
Axis 3 — Life-Context Cluster (4 values): sourced from Occupation and Working_status, grouped into four broad clusters that determine tone and framing rather than content.
5 × 3 × 4 = 60 combinatorial personas, + 6 cross-cutting personas = 66 total personas.
A person's underlying life-need domain is not the same as their app behaviour — someone can behave like a "Scheme Seeker" while their confirmed need sits in Health, Skills, or Agriculture, since all three domains include scheme/benefit-type questions in the survey (H3, S4, A6). That's why every Behaviour × Domain pairing is meaningful rather than arbitrary.

4. Cross-Cutting / Special Personas (6)
These sit outside the 5×3×4 grid — they're triggered by a data condition rather than a combination of the three axes, and can apply on top of any of the 60 core personas above.
ID
Persona
Trigger (schema condition)
How it changes the approach
X1
New / Dormant Signup
Near-zero values across content_score, scheme_score, job_score, service_score, and minimal engagement_time_min, regardless of primary_category.
Activation sequence, not a nurture or conversion sequence — first goal is a single opened notification and one completed in-app action.
X2
Multi-Domain Achiever
primary_category = High Converter with comparably elevated scores across more than one domain-relevant signal — behaviourally active across Health, Skills and Agriculture rather than one.
Rotate domain focus notification-by-notification instead of committing to a single lean; track which domain each message converts on to progressively narrow the persona.
X3
Incomplete-Profile User
Missing values in key classifying fields (age, Occupation, Working_status).
Fall back to primary_category + notification_tag only (Rules 1–3 from the assignment logic); avoid domain- or life-context-specific claims until more profile data is captured.
X4
Economically Vulnerable Overlay
bpl_category = True, or personal/family income at the lowest recorded bands.
Cross-cutting priority flag applied on top of any of the 60 core personas — benefit/subsidy and free-service messaging is promoted ahead of paid or optional content for this user, regardless of their core persona.
X5
Minority-Language User
preferred_language outside the three dominant languages (e.g. Punjabi, Tamil, Bengali, Assamese, Malayalam, Telugu, Odia, Gujarati, Kannada).
Route to translation/localisation review before send; do not assume template copy translates cleanly — flag for manual QA given small volume per language.
X6
Urban-Context User
district ∈ metro/urban areas (e.g. Mumbai City, Mumbai Suburban, Thane) rather than the predominantly rural district set.
Adjust "nearby service/camp" content to urban-density assumptions (more facility options, shorter distances, different transport framing) rather than the rural-default copy used elsewhere.
5. Assignment Flow — Placing Every User
Step 1: Assign the Behavioural Segment (B1–B5) from primary_category — always populated, guarantees every user lands in one of five buckets before any further refinement.
Step 2: Check for X1 (dormant) or X3 (incomplete profile) conditions — if triggered, stop here and use the reduced-data version of the persona until more signal is available.
Step 3: Assign the Life-Context Cluster (LC1–LC4) from Occupation / Working_status.
Step 4: Assign the Life-Need Domain (D1–D3) — provisionally from Occupation/age/family fields as outlined in the prior framework, then confirmed/overridden once individual survey responses are joined by user_id.
Step 5: Apply any remaining overlays that fit — X2 (multi-domain), X4 (BPL/economic vulnerability), X5 (minority language), X6 (urban context) — these can stack on top of the core 60.
Because Steps 1 and 2 alone already guarantee a placement for every user, the 60-persona grid and the 6 overlays exist purely to sharpen an already-complete assignment — no user can fall outside the taxonomy.
6. Note on Scope
As before, this taxonomy is built from column names and the survey's question/bucket structure only — no individual user records were reviewed to produce it. The Life-Need Domain assignment in particular is a schema-level placeholder (Occupation/age-based) until a per-user survey response table, keyed on user_id, is joined to the profile data. At that point Step 4 should switch from the proxy rule to the actual signal values captured in the questionnaire.
